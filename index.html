方法 fāngfǎ · how to learn

Little, daily, and it sticks

Básico 1 is small and finishable. What decides whether it sticks is not how long you study in one go, but that you touch it every day — even for ten minutes.

10 min a day, your floor 1 class a week · Mon 19:00 7 review boxes

1 Your 10-minute day

The floor is what you do on a bad day. Anything beyond it is a bonus.

  1. 4min
    Cards first 复习 fùxí

    Open Today and answer the cards that are due. Say each one out loud before you flip it.

  2. 3min
    Listen 听 tīng

    Play one short track or one listening drill. Don't look at the page the first time through.

  3. 3min
    Write or say 写 xiě · 说 shuō

    Work on this week's homework, or write 2–3 characters by hand following the stroke order.

Bad day? Just the cards. Four minutes still counts, and it keeps the streak and the spacing working. Missed a day? Don't double up — pick up where you are.

2 The week around your class

One online class a week, Mondays 19:00 (São Paulo time). Study is built around it.

  • MonClass. Take notes of anything new; ask about whatever you didn't catch.
  • TueWithin a day: look at what was taught and write the homework down. Memory is strongest right after class.
  • Wed–FriThe 10-minute day. Homework in small bites, not the night before.
  • SatOne slightly longer session if you have it (20–30 min): exercises or a full audio track.
  • SunLight cards only, then check the Schedule for what Monday brings.

3 How the cards come back

Each character or word is a card that lives in a box. A new card starts in box 1. Every box has a waiting time before the card returns:

✓
Got it

The card moves up one box and waits longer before coming back.

↺
Again

The card goes back to the start and returns soon. That's the system working, not failing.

Try it: pretend you're answering one card.

你好nǐ hǎo

Box 1 · due now

  • Only taught material. A card enters your queue after it has been taught in class — never because it appears later in the book.
  • "Known" means a card has survived three correct reviews in a row (box 4 or higher).
  • Why it works: you see a card just as you're about to forget it, so each review buys a longer gap. Cards you know cost almost nothing; the effort goes where you need it.
  • A day's new cards and reviews have a cap, so a big backlog never becomes a wall.

4 Getting the most from the online class

From the Instituto Confúcio guide Aproveitando melhor as aulas online.

Before class
  • Check your internet, and that audio and video work on the device you'll use.
  • Pick a quiet spot if you can.
  • Tell the people you live with when your class is.
  • If your webcam is on, classmates and the teacher can see you.
  • Be on time.
During class
  • Avoid distractions: pause social media and messaging apps, turn the TV off.
  • Keep your microphone muted while others speak — many open mics make noise. Unmute only to talk.
  • Have a question? Ask the teacher, or write it in the chat.
Between classes
  • Set aside time to review past lessons — it helps you remember.
  • Build a study routine and do the homework.
  • Watch films and series and listen to music in Mandarin; try learning apps; ask your teacher for tips.
  • The Instituto Confúcio blog has culture posts and tips on digital libraries with Chinese-language material.
  • Learn to type in pīnyīn and in hànzì (characters) on your computer and phone — the Instituto has tutorials.

6 Small habits that help

  • Tie it to something you already do. Cards with the morning coffee beats "I'll find time".
  • Say it aloud. Tones live in your mouth and ears, not just your eyes.
  • Write the hànzì by hand now and then, stroke by stroke. It fixes shapes in memory.
  • Repeat, don't rush ahead. The course is small enough to finish solidly — solid is the goal.
